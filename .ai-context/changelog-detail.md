# Detailed change history

Every change, newest first, with the numbers that decided it. **This is the regression
suite.** This project has repeatedly been re-broken by reasoning about the code instead of
measuring it, so each entry records what was measured before and after — those figures are
how you tell whether a later change has quietly undone an earlier one.

Read this when you are about to change something and want to know what it cost to get
right. For *what shipped when*, see [`../CHANGELOG.md`](../CHANGELOG.md).

Entries predate the release-note split of 2026-08-22 and are not grouped by version: many
landed on the same day, and the useful axis here is the measurement, not the tag.

| Date | Change |
|---|---|
| 2026-08-22 | **The hub grew until it was half the disc, and the logo grew with it.** `HOLE` is 0.3 precisely so the hub stays the same FRACTION of the disc at any size -- its own note records that a fixed r0 once gave "a 32% hole at full size and a 69% one when filtered down". Letting the band balancer grow r0 freely to hit a thickness ratio reintroduced exactly that: measured **47.3% of the disc on a 450-note vault and 58.2% at 10,000**, r0 running 2.05x and 2.4x its formula value. The centre logo is drawn at half the hole, so it visibly grew -- 160px on one balancer target, 130px on the next, for a reason that has nothing to do with the logo. The hole share is now priced into the search (`HOLE_MAX` 0.36, penalised rather than forbidden) and comes out at **35.4% and 34.4%**. The cost is real and worth stating: the 450-note vault's thickness ratio slips from 0.53 to 0.40 against a 0.55 target, because keeping the hole honest is worth more than the last 0.13 of ring balance. |
| 2026-08-22 | **The logo is a fixed 128px, capped by the hole.** It was purely a fraction of the hub, so it changed size whenever the layout did -- once r0 became something the balancer solves for, the mark was resizing with the folder split. `LOGO_OF_HOLE` survives as a CEILING rather than the rule: zooming out shrinks the hole in pixels, and a genuinely fixed size would spill the mark under the innermost notes. |
| 2026-08-22 | **The note-aim check is density-aware instead of flaky.** At 10,000 notes the dots are ~2.5px in radius and the most isolated one has ~6px of clearance, so aiming at its centre lands on the dot some runs and in the gap on others -- the same build passed at 5.2px and missed at 6.1px. Below 10px of clearance a miss is now REPORTED as too dense to aim rather than failed. A flaky check is worse than an honest gap: it trains you to re-run instead of to read. |
| 2026-08-22 | **The split and the hub radius are searched JOINTLY, and the target moved to 0.55.** Scoring a split at the base radius and only then solving r0 is two-stage and measurably wrong: on the 450-note vault it chose 2/4 rows for a **0.27** ratio when 3/3 reaches 0.80 -- closer to the target -- because the split had been scored against a radius the layout then moved away from. `share()` depends only on the split, so it runs once per split and the radius scan reuses it: cost is now the best over the radius range rather than the value at one point in it. Retargeted from 0.75 to 0.55, which reads as the outer ring carrying the disc rather than two rings competing. Final: **0.53 at 450 notes (3/4 rows), 0.55 at 10,000 (12/17)** -- both on target, where before the joint search they were 0.27 and 0.56. |
| 2026-08-22 | **Balanced bands, and four ways of getting it wrong first.** Which ring a group sits in was decided by size alone -- every group too small to earn its minimum wedge went inner -- which on a 10,000-note fixture gave **35 rows inner against 9 outer**. Groups are now moved between rings to target an inner thickness of 0.75x the outer. The mistakes, in order: (1) the balancer sat above the declaration of `r0`, so `var` hoisting handed it undefined, `rowsNeeded` returned 1 for every group, and it was a **silent no-op** -- 35/9 became 34/9 and the suite stayed green because nothing asserted the thing the feature was for; (2) it optimised `rows x pitch` while the eye sees `(rows-1) x pitch`, a fifth of the answer on a shallow band -- it believed 0.40 where the disc showed 0.32; (3) single-move greedy descent got **stuck at 0.32** on a vault where a two-group move reaches 0.80, so splits are now enumerated exhaustively when the movable set is small; (4) it bought even rings by **exiling small folders to the rim**, where a three-note folder is three lonely dots holding open a 6-degree wedge. Small folders are pinned inner now -- and the pin threshold had to become **absolute** (fewer than 10 notes) rather than `smallAt`, which scales with the vault and made a 148-note folder "small" at 10k, nailing 32 cells into the hub and leaving no assignment that could recover. Final: **0.80 at 450 notes, 0.72 at 10,000**, inner thinner than outer in both, no small folder outside. |
| 2026-08-22 | **The hub radius is solved, not assumed.** `r0` came from `HOLE` and the note count and knew nothing about how many notes ended up pinned inside it. With small folders unable to leave, moving groups is not enough: the inner band is a full circle shared among its groups, so adding a big group takes arc *away* from the small ones and costs them rows. The radius is scanned outward to 3x and the best kept -- bounded because past that the hole stops being a hole and becomes the composition. |
| 2026-08-22 | **Unlinked notes became a group.** They were sunflower-packed into the hub hole, which put them outside every rule the rest of the language follows: no wedge, no legend row, no filter, no count, and their folder silently under-reported its size. `groupOf` returns `(unlinked)` for degree 0, so they flow through colour, legend, filtering and band assignment untouched. `hubPositions` deleted with it. |
| 2026-08-22 | **Wedge gaps scale with density.** `SLICE_GAP` of 2 degrees was tuned where rows sit ~28px apart; the disc's pixel size is fixed, so 10,000 notes close the lattice around a seam that does not close and it reads as a missing slice. Scaled to nothing by 10,000 notes, sub-gaps riding the same factor so the hierarchy between the two does not invert. Note COUNT is the proxy on purpose: the radius is pinned by the normalisation box, and using it would be circular since `gapFor` is called to compute the reference width the radius comes from. |

| 2026-08-22 | **Both rings rotated back by half a gap, so the wrap gap is CENTRED on 12 o'clock instead of starting there.** The allocator places a full gap before the first group and sweep 0 is 12 o'clock, so the gap between the last group and the first spanned `[0, gap]` -- 12 o'clock sat on its leading edge, which reads as the disc being rotated half a gap clockwise. Applied at the sweep-to-angle conversion rather than by changing the leading offset: that offset arithmetic is what keeps every term continuous when a group fades out, and it is not worth disturbing for a constant rotation. `gap` there is the BAND's own already-clamped gap, so one line rotates each ring by its own half-gap. Constant, deliberately not presence-weighted -- anything weighted there would rotate the ring as notes arrive, which is exactly the bug class the leading-offset note describes. Measured: outer wrap-gap centre **+1.019 deg to +0.019 deg**, inner **+2.608 deg to +1.607 deg**, every note in both bands moving by exactly **1.0 deg** (`SLICE_GAP` 2 deg, so `gap/2`). The inner band's residual is not an error and not a rotation the change missed -- it is that a 1-note wedge centres its note ~3 deg from each edge, so note positions do not mark the boundary there; the outer band, whose rows are full, lands on 12 o'clock to 0.019 deg. |
| 2026-08-22 | **The lattice invariant is checked after all — I had written it off too early.** It was listed as uncoverable because the literal form, "radius is base + an integer row x SP", needs `SP`, `INNER_SCALE`, `UNIT` and `geomLock`, none of which are on the debug handle. The EQUIVALENT claim needs none of them: *the distinct radii within one band must be evenly spaced*, from node positions plus `buildWedgePlan(false)` for band membership. It is also stricter, catching a band whose spacing drifted and not just a fractional radius. Measured inner **2 rows at 128, spread 0** and outer **7 rows at 160, spread 0** -- and those gaps are the constants themselves, `UNIT x SP` and `UNIT x INNER_SCALE`. Excludes notes below full alpha (mid-cascade radii are legitimately fractional -- that is what `rowsOf` is for) and degree-0 notes (sunflower-packed into the hub hole, never on the lattice; moot on this vault's 0 orphans but not on one with them). 15 checks now; the only manual invariant left is the per-frame animation step. Sabotage-tested, the same way the hook was: nudging one note 40 units -- a quarter of a row -- takes the outer band from `7 rows, spread 0, even` to `8 rows, gap 40, spread 120, NOT even`. |

| 2026-08-22 | **`scripts/smoke.mjs`: every automatable invariant in one command.** The documented failure mode of this repo is reasoning about the code instead of measuring it, and until now the measuring was something a person had to remember to do, one console command at a time. | 2026-08-22 | **The highlight ramp is PER NOTE, not one global scalar.** Highlights are additive -- two groups can be lit at once, a subfolder can be lit inside an already-lit group, and a heatmap hover swaps one whole set for another -- so a single ramp would pop the second set in at whatever value the first had left it, and could not fade one set out while another fades in. `hl[id]` follows the pattern `alpha` already establishes. Everything about the treatment now keys on `hl[id]` rather than on `isHighlighted(id)` -- on the way out the predicate is already false while the ramp is still above zero, so keying the halo on it dropped the ring on the fade's first frame and left a shrinking bare dot. The ring is MIXED from the note's own colour instead of alpha-faded, so it emerges from the dot rather than ghosting over it and stays a solid hex, which is the only thing Sigma's colour parser is reliable about. Started from `afterRender` behind a signature of what is highlighted, so none of the six places that write the highlight set has to remember to kick it. |
| 2026-08-22 | **Hovering a note is animated instead of snapping.** It used to switch on one frame: everything else to `--dim`, the label with it. That is the same discrete step this project exists to remove from the layout, and it read the same way — as the disc flinching rather than responding. `hoverT` ramps over **150ms** (a third of the click tween, because this fires on every note the pointer crosses) and everything visible is a function of it: the dim, the lift (`HOVER_GROW` 0.45), the edge highlight, and the label, which waits until `hoverT > 0.5` so the box does not land on notes that are still at full colour. Wall-clock and frame-clamped like every other animation here, so a stalled frame stretches it rather than leaping. Mixing is sRGB, not OKLab: this runs per node per frame and both endpoints are known, so a perceptual path buys nothing visible and costs two cbrt per channel. Measured — ramp in `0.027 0.172 0.349 0.528 0.706 0.884 1`, out `0.98 0.776 0.687 0.509 0.331 0.153 0`, a distant node reduced to exactly `#2a2a28`, and the hovered note grown 4.45 -> 6.52. The hovered id is released by the tween **at zero**, not in `leaveNode` — clearing it there empties `focusSet()` on that frame and the fade-out is never drawn. |
