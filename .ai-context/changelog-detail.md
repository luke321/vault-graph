# Detailed change history

Every change, newest first, with the numbers that decided it. **This is the regression
suite.** This project has repeatedly been re-broken by reasoning about the code instead of
measuring it, so each entry records what was measured before and after — those figures are
how you tell whether a later change has quietly undone an earlier one.

Read this when you are about to change something and want to know what it cost to get
right. For *what shipped when*, see [`../CHANGELOG.md`](../CHANGELOG.md).

Entries predate the release-note split of 2026-08-22 and are not grouped by version: many
landed on the same day, and the useful axis here is the measurement, not the tag.

| 2026-08-23 | **The gap that was too wide was the CHANNEL, and two rounds of shrinking other things proved it.** Channel over an ordinary step, measured on three vaults: **1.60, 1.72, 1.52** -- and almost none of it the seam. Taking the seam from 24 units to 12 moved that number from 1.60 to **1.60**; adding an excess on top of the existing margin made it **1.90**, because the margin plus each end note own radius already WAS the excess. The quantity to name is therefore the channel: what a boundary has over one note-to-note step, with half a pitch per side and nothing added as the zero point (two wedges each holding their end note half a step in from their own edge put those notes exactly one step apart, which is invisible). Scaled as one quantity by the rows falloff: **1.32 / 1.23 / 1.07** against 1.30 / 1.18 / 1.00 asked for. Verified neutral first -- at EXCESS_KEEP 0 it reproduces 1.60 / 1.72 / 1.52 exactly, so the new phrasing is the old geometry until the constant moves. The end margin also stopped ADDING each note radius and corrects for how it differs from a typical one instead: adding it bought edge symmetry at a cost of ~126 units of width at every boundary, and the difference buys the same symmetry for nothing on average. An ordinary step widened as a side effect, **150/143/156 -> 155/156/163** -- margin the wedges are not spending is room the notes get. |
| 2026-08-23 | **Dot size was fighting the camera, and no seam could have survived it.** Sigma's default `zoomToSizeRatioFunction` is `Math.sqrt`, so a node's drawn radius goes as `1/sqrt(ratio)` while its POSITION goes as `1/ratio` -- the two diverge on every zoom, and no choice of size holds a dot at a fixed fraction of the gap to its neighbour. Measured drawn diameter over row pitch: **0.76 at rest, 1.44 three notches in, 3.51 at the far end** -- dots swallowing their neighbours. Set to identity, so sizes follow the same law as positions, and the figures handed in are pinned to the lattice once rather than re-derived per frame: **0.787 / 0.786 / 0.786 across a 21x zoom range.** Two earlier attempts were measured with `getNodeDisplayData().size`, which is the reducer's output and NOT the drawn radius -- both reported a flat 0.786 while the screen showed dots touching. `renderer.scaleSize()` is the honest number; the label placer goes through it too, having been out by a factor of 20 zoomed in. |
| 2026-08-23 | **The size floor stopped tracking the lattice, and that was the dots overlapping.** `SIZE_FLOOR` 0.45 existed so dots could not vanish, calibrated against vaults whose rows land 22-30px apart; a 10,000-note disc fitted to the stage puts them **4.5px** apart, where the honest scale is 0.16 and the floor held it at 0.45 -- **2.8x too big for the lattice.** Measured: biggest dot **175 units of radius against a 160-unit row pitch**, 349 across, and the MEDIAN dot 192 across. Replaced by handling the two ends separately, because they want opposite things: the biggest dot is capped at a fraction of the pitch (or notes collide), the smallest is floored in PIXELS (or notes disappear), linear between, so the degree ordering shows wherever there is room and the range flattens rather than overflowing where there is not. Biggest diameter **349 -> 126** against a 160 pitch. A NODE_MAX pixel ceiling survived one round and had to go with it: it froze dots at 11px while the lattice kept growing, which read as swelling and then dwindling. |
| 2026-08-23 | **Every dot is sized against its own nearest neighbour, measured rather than assumed.** The row pitch is a constant; the along-row pitch is not, because a wedge's end margins come out of the same circumference its notes are spread across -- so a band carrying many wedges packs its rows tighter, and dots sized against the row pitch alone came out **126 units of diameter against a 120-unit step**. One figure for the whole disc was tried first and is worse: it has to be the tightest spot anywhere, and it shrank every dot for the sake of a handful, **0.79 -> 0.24 diameter over spacing disc-wide.** Per note now, keyed by id, taking the smaller of its two sides, and measured **across wedge boundaries as well as within them** -- those are the notes with the least room, and missing them left boundary dots overlapping by 27 units on the 1402-note vault (now +9). The 10k keeps a **-17 unit** worst case where the pixel floor wins over the cap; that is 0.48px on screen, against dots that would otherwise not be drawn at all. |
| 2026-08-23 | **The seam: a width rather than an angle, one width for every boundary, parallel-sided, measured to the dots' edges, and falling off as a band gets deeper.** Five changes, each from looking at it. (1) A degree is not a width -- it buys arc in proportion to radius, so the same 2 degrees was 75 units on a 454-note vault and 129 on a 1402-note one, which is where "the gaps are too big" came from. (2) Group boundaries take the SUB-wedge width, since the colours and the legend already say which is which and the wide version read as a missing slice. (3) Sizing it as a width and dividing by each note's own radius makes the channel's edges genuinely parallel: a point at radius r offset by w/2r sits at perpendicular distance w/2 for any r. Measured on the 1402-note vault, boundary arc at the inner row against the outer: **214 -> 205 units across a 4.37x change in radius**, where a constant angle would have given 4.37x. (4) The margin is measured to the note's EDGE rather than its centre: dot radius runs 4x from leaf to hub, so equal centre distances put edges visibly unequal distances from the boundary -- reported from a screenshot of the 12 o'clock axis with a hub on one side and a leaf on the other. (5) It falls off as `(5/rows)^1.5`, fitted to three judged points: half the base seam at 5 outer rows, a quarter at 9, none to speak of at 23. Final widths: **12, 4 and 1 graph units.** Third rule to stand where `gapScale`'s note-count ramp used to, and the first measured against the geometry rather than a proxy for it. |

| 2026-08-23 | **A 500-note vault and a 1500-note vault filtered down to 500 drew different discs, because the lattice spacing was a constant (github#13).** Reported as an impression -- "a 1500-note vault toggled down to 500 will render differently" -- and it was exactly right. `SP`, the lattice spacing that sets both the radial gap between rows and the tangential gap along them, was a hard 1. With the normalisation box pinned to the full-vault extent and the camera still, screen pixels per lattice unit is a constant, so **screen row pitch was a constant per vault**: measured 19.481px at every filter state of a 500-note vault and 12.064px at every state of a 1500-note one, while filtering 503 notes down to 62 moved the median dot 4.254px -> 4.208px and the hole from 32.8% to 43.9% of the disc. That last number is the exact bug `r0`'s own formula comment was written to prevent -- pinning `r0` into `geomLock` reintroduced it for every filtered view. **The solve is exact, not a fudge.** A lattice of spacing `s` holds `1/s^2` notes per unit area, so an annulus with its hole at `HOLE*R` holds `n = pi*R^2*(1-HOLE^2)/s^2`; setting that equal to the full-vault `R` gives `s = sqrt(n_full / n_visible)`, on the same uniform-density assumption the `r0` formula already makes. After: `pitchPx * sqrt(shown)` spread **2.85x -> 1.10x** (500-note) and 2.82x -> 1.09x (1500-note), and every state below the density cap is **exactly** 436.919 / 467.219 -- invariant rather than approximately so, with the whole residual in the capped step. Median dot 4.238px -> 10.854px; hole holds 0.304-0.328. `r0` needed no change at all: pinned at `HOLE * maxR` it holds the hole at a constant share once the disc keeps its radius. **Three things were got wrong on the way and are worth recording.** (1) A **correction pass** that fed `maxR` back and scaled `SP` until it landed on the locked extent made it *worse* -- spread 1.10x -> 1.15x -- because it drove `SP` back to 1 nearly everywhere. The reason contradicts the premise the work started from: **filtering barely moves the disc's radius.** `maxR` is the max over cells, so the deepest survivor still reaches the rim -- `reach` was 1.000 at 481, 465 and 382 of 503 notes showing, i.e. there was never any empty margin to reclaim. Filtering makes the disc *sparser* at a radius that hardly moves, and that radius is quantised in whole rows and already flush with the box, so 2.3% more spacing buys 7% more radius and no spacing exists between "no change" and "one row over". (2) **`HL_PUSH` was scaled by `SP` and should not be.** It is quoted in rows, so scaling looked right, but it was sized as a fraction of the *radius* ("0.9 rows on a ~13.3-row disc is 6.8%") and the radius is what the solve holds still -- it is the row count that falls. At the cap the disc is ~5 rows deep, so a scaled push would have been 18% of it and a highlighted note would have protruded off the stage. (3) The **cascade must be handed the spacing**, not left to derive one: deriving it from `planTotal` mid-animation samples alpha-weighted membership, which slides every frame, and the biggest single-frame radial step went **0 -> 94 units against a row of 160**. Fixed the way rows already were -- both endpoint packings built from binary presence, per-frame value interpolated between them on the same clock as `rowsAt` -- back to 0. **`fitRatio()`'s upper clamp came off 1**, which was correct only while the disc could exclusively shrink; a spread lattice puts the outermost row a few percent past the lock (measured 1.079) and that is disc, not margin, so fit was framing a filtered disc with its rim cut off. **Continuous on purpose.** The retired `REPACK_BELOW` mechanism switched plan basis at 55% and all three of its recorded failures were failures of the *threshold*; a smooth function of visible weight has no side to be on, and it keeps both plan invariants for free -- a departing note sits at weight 0 and adds nothing to `planTotal`, and the lean and padded plans each derive it from their own. Two new checks in `scripts/smoke.mjs` and a new `scripts/measure-density.mjs`, which is the instrument this was built with rather than an afterthought. |
